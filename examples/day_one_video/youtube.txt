TITLE
Day One: You Spend It Trying to Find Out What's Happening

DESCRIPTION
The lights go out at 11:48 on a Thursday and the first thing you lose is not the power. It is the ability to find out what is happening.

A message from a number you have not spoken to in two years. A link that does not load. Forty messages in a minute asking the same question. A nine-second video too dark to prove anything, and a second one, and a third that is already a hoax. You spend an hour and ten minutes in bed on your phone trying to find out, and it is the last hour you will ever spend like that.

This is Day One. No monsters. A locked door, a street with one car left in it, a woman walking past her own front door, and at eight in the morning a neighbour knocking with the same sentence on a forty-second timer.

What survives the first night is not courage. It is a decision made in four seconds, and then the patience to sit still with it.

Day Two starts when he stops.

Sources: Ready.gov and FEMA power-outage guidance, CDC emergency water supply guidance, and the disaster-behaviour literature on panic and milling (Quarantelli; Tierney).
