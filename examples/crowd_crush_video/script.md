# The Crowd Doesn't Trample You

*Saiki Survives — Episode 01*

The crowd does not trample you. It kills you standing up. Upright. Awake. In the middle of a crowd that has stopped being a crowd. Nobody in it is running. Nobody is even screaming.

---

You cannot see the danger. Crowds look identical right up until they turn. So forget the noise, and count bodies. A safe crowd gives you a square meter. Two people, and you can still walk. Five, and you choose nothing. Six, and the crowd becomes a fluid.

---

One test works even in the dark. Are you being touched on both shoulders, front and back, at the same moment? Then the density around you is six. That is not discomfort. That is your margin closing. Leave then, not later.

---

Here is what actually kills people. Not trampling. Compression. Two hundred pounds of crowd on your ribs is more than your chest can lift. Your diaphragm cannot pull down. Your lungs get no room to open. This is compressive asphyxia, and it takes minutes. The coroners find almost no injuries. Just people who were standing up.

---

The word stampede is wrong. Stampedes are for animals. Crowd scientist Keith Still put it plainly: people do not die because they panic. They panic because they are dying.

---

Past six per square meter, physics takes over. The crowd stops flowing and starts sloshing. Pressure waves travel through it like water, moving hundreds of people at once, whether they agree or not. Then one person goes down. In that density, nobody behind them can stop. The falling spreads like dominoes.

---

You have four moves. Learn them now. One. Make a cage around your chest. Hands up in front, like a boxer, elbows braced, one foot forward, knees soft so you can absorb a shove. That buys you a centimeter of air. A centimeter is the whole difference.

---

Two. Do not fight the pressure. You will lose, and you will be exhausted when you need the energy most. Float with it instead, and wait for the pressure to stall. In that pause, step diagonally. Never forward. Always toward the edge.

---

Three. If you drop your phone, leave it. Bending down is how people die on their feet. You will not get back up. Also, stop screaming. Air is your clock. Lift your chin toward cooler air.

---

Four. If you go down, protect organs and make yourself small. Curl onto your left side, knees to chest, arms over your head. Get up the instant there is room, and crawl toward the edge, not the front.

---

The last move is the only one that scales. Choose your position before you arrive. Stand at the rim of the crowd, know two exits, and never in the funnel by the gate.

---

A crowd of calm people can kill you. A crowd of frightened people usually will not. Density is the only number that matters. So watch the gap between shoulders, and when it disappears, you leave. Not running. Just gone. Saiki survives. See you next time.
