# NINE FLOORS — v1 draft

You are the caretaker of a nine-storey block, and you live in the basement.

Every morning you do the same six things: kettle, radio, shoes, keys, bins, pump.

This morning the fifth thing is where it stops. The bins are out. The street is quiet. A man at the corner is standing in the road, not crossing it.

You wave at him. He doesn't wave back. You decide he's a delivery driver.

At seven twenty the water stops.

You go down to the plant room and look at the gauge. The tank is fine. The mains is not.

Nine floors above you, an old woman opens her door and asks if you have any water. You give her your last bottle and tell her you'll sort it.

If you want water in this building today, you have to open the valve in the basement plant room by hand.

You get your torch. You get the big key. You go to the stairs.

The stairwell is dark. Halfway down, a man is standing in the corner facing the wall.

You say: you alright, mate?

He turns around.

You go back up. You are faster than him. You get to your floor and the door is locked, because you left your keys inside.

You go up instead. Floor four, five, six. You can hear him on the stairs behind you, not hurrying.

You reach the top and there is nowhere to go but the roof door, and it opens, and the roof is empty and grey.

You sit down and wait.

Later the noise stops and you go back down, all nine floors, in the dark, to the plant room.

You open the valve. The pump starts. The water comes back on.

You stand there and listen to the pipes fill.

That's it. That's the day.
