You wake before the alarm, because the room is too bright.
The little clock on the wall shows nothing at all.
Your phone is a black mirror beside your hand.
The fridge has stopped humming in the next room.
You lie still and listen to a silence that feels held.
Somewhere far off, a car alarm starts and stops.
You swing your feet onto the cold floor.
The air tastes like dust and cold smoke.
You pull the curtain back with two fingers.
The street below is empty in the wrong way.
A delivery van sits sideways across the road.
Its back doors hang open onto the pavement.
A traffic light blinks amber over nothing.
No one walks. No one drives. Nothing moves at all.
Then one person walks into the middle of your view.
He moves slowly, dragging one foot behind the other.
His head hangs forward onto his chest.
He stops in the sun and does not look up.
You tell yourself that he is sick.
A second figure appears from behind the van.
She moves the same way, with the same tilted head.
The two of them stand ten feet apart and do not speak.
A door opens somewhere below you in the building.
Somebody screams once, and the scream ends early.
You step back from the window and hold the curtain.
Three knocks land on your front door.
You do not move.
Three knocks come again, slower this time.
You cross the room without letting the floor take your weight.
The peephole shows the hallway in a circle of grey light.
A man from downstairs fills that little circle.
His face is slack, turned slightly away from the glass.
One of his hands rests flat against your door.
He wears the same jacket he wore yesterday.
There is a dark stain across the front of his shirt.
You cannot remember his name, only his floor.
He leans in until his eye fills the peephole.
You step back and put your hand over your mouth.
The knocking starts again and does not stop.
The door shudders in its frame but holds.
You can open the door and be kind.
Or you can leave the door closed and stay alive.
You choose the door closed.
You back into the kitchen and finally let yourself breathe.
The water in the tap runs for three seconds, then dies.
You fill every bottle you can find before it stops.
Your hands shake so hard that you spill half of one.
The radio gives you static between two dead stations.
Then a voice says to stay indoors and wait.
The voice repeats itself until the batteries fade.
In the hallway outside, the knocking has changed.
It is lower now, at the neighbour's door.
You hear wood crack once, and then a long quiet.
You take a backpack from the bottom of the closet.
Into it go the bottles, a torch, and one warm coat.
You add a lighter, a length of rope, and your keys.
You leave the photograph face down on the shelf.
You pull on shoes with thick soles and tie them twice.
At the front door, the peephole is dark now.
You put your eye to it and see nothing at all.
Something is pressed against the other side of the glass.
You do not touch the handle.
The window in the bedroom opens onto the fire escape.
That is the way out, and you take it.
Cold air hits your face as the frame lifts.
You swing one leg over the sill and then the other.
The metal stairs complain under your weight.
Halfway down, you look up at your own window.
The curtain is still moving where you let it go.
You reach the alley and put your back to the wall.
The alley is narrow, wet, and full of bins.
A figure stands at the far end with its back to you.
It is perfectly still, facing the bright street.
You take one step, then stop, then take another.
A can shifts under your foot and rings on the stone.
The figure at the far end turns its head.
You hold your breath and do not run.
It takes two steps toward the sound and then stops.
A bus roars past the mouth of the alley, going fast.
The figure turns toward the noise and follows it.
You wait until the street is empty again.
Then you walk, slowly, the way it walks.
At the corner you see three more of them in the square.
They move between parked cars without hurry.
A shop window is broken and the street is full of glass.
You keep to the shade under the awnings.
Your bag is heavy and the straps bite your shoulders.
You choose the road that climbs away from the city.
You decide to walk until the buildings thin out.
You decide not to look back until you reach the ridge.
The sun is lower by the time the road turns to dirt.
Behind you, the city is quiet under a column of smoke.
A single light still burns in one high window.
You cannot tell if it is yours.
You do not go back to check.
You have made one decision today and it holds.
The door stays closed.
You keep moving until the light is gone.
And when the dark comes, you are still outside, and still walking.
