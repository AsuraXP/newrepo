# Day One

> Night one does not kill you. Day one does.

Third floor. Second floor. You take the stairs two at a time. You do not look back. Looking back costs a step, and you need every step. She is behind you. She is not running. She is walking, and she is keeping up. The fire door at the bottom is shut. You hit it with your shoulder and it gives. You pull it shut behind you and stand in the dark with your hand on the bar, breathing.

**Day one.**

Eleven hours earlier you were in bed with the sound off. A message came from a number you had not spoken to in two years. Two words and a link. The link did not load. You tried again. Then the group chat went. Forty messages in a minute, all asking the same thing.

Then the videos started. The first one was nine seconds long and too dark to prove anything. The second was clearer. A man on a pavement, standing up, then not standing up. The third was a hoax. Somebody had already made a hoax, and now you could not tell. You watched them until your eyes hurt. You spent an hour and ten minutes trying to find out what was happening. You did not get up once.

At 12:58 the signal went. One bar. Then nothing. The phone stayed bright and became useless. The fridge stopped. The router light went out. The lift did not come. That was the moment the flat stopped being a flat and became the only building you had.

You went to the window. The street was empty except for one car stopped at an angle, driver's door open, headlights still on. Nobody came back for it. Down the end of the road a woman was walking. She walked past her own front door and did not stop. Her left arm hung wrong. It swung a beat behind the rest of her.

Two floors down, somebody started calling a name. It was a woman's voice, out in the hall, patient, the way you call a child who has wandered off. She called it eleven times. On the twelfth she stopped in the middle of the word. You put your back against your door and you did not move. Then something hit your door. Once. Not a knock. A weight.

**At eight the next morning, somebody knocked. Three times. Evenly spaced. The way a neighbour knocks. It was Mr Adeyemi from 4B. You knew his voice. Hello. It's Mr Adeyemi. Can you let me in. You did not move. He said it again. Same words, same pause in the same place. Every forty seconds. For an hour.**

You looked through the peephole. He was standing too close to the door. His head was tilted. His mouth was open and it stayed open. Behind him, at the end of the corridor, another one stood facing the wall. You backed away and you did not make a sound.

You chained the door. Then you went and got the furniture. The bookcase across the frame. The table on its side. The chair wedged under the handle. You worked quietly, which took four times as long. It took you an hour to turn the hallway into a dead end. Then you sat down in it and looked at what you had built. Two locks, a door, and a metre of pine between you and the landing. You have never been so glad of a bookcase.

From the toolbox you took the longest pipe you had. It was heavy and cold and it made you feel better. You put it by the door and sat down where you could reach it.

By nine the knocking had stopped. You listened for five minutes. Nothing. The corridor was empty when you looked. Adeyemi's door was open. His light was on. You should have stayed inside. You know that now.

You pulled the bookcase back and you opened your door twelve inches and listened. Nothing. You stepped out onto the landing in your socks. The corridor smelled of something you did not want to name. At the far end, the woman from the hall was standing facing the wall.

She had been facing that wall for an hour. You took one step back. The floorboard under your foot made a sound like a gunshot. She turned around. She turned her whole body first, from the hips, and her head came last.

You ran. Down the landing, past Adeyemi's open door, into the stairwell. Two floors. You took them three steps at a time. She came after you, and she did not hurry, and she did not fall behind. You hit the fire door at the bottom with your shoulder. It gave, and you pulled it shut behind you.

You went up the other stairwell and got into your flat and chained the door. Then you stood in the hallway and listened to her arrive. She walked into the door. Not fast. She walked into it and stayed there. The chain held. The wood held.

You turned around, and there was another one in your kitchen. It came through the bathroom window. The one you never locked. It was four feet away and it was already reaching. You had the pipe in your hand before you decided anything. One swing. It went down and it did not get up. You stood over it and shook, and then you stepped over it and went for the window.

Out of the bathroom window onto the fire escape. Three floors up, cold metal, wind. You climbed. The roof was flat and grey and empty and it was the best thing you had ever seen. You sat with your back to a chimney and looked down at the street. They were standing in it. Dozens of them. Still. Facing the building. Not one of them was looking up.

Your phone had twelve percent left and no signal and no use. You put it in your pocket and you stayed where you were. You were alive, and it was still day one. Tomorrow they will still be there. Tomorrow you will have to go down. Day two starts when one of them looks up.
