# Night One

> The grid does not fail all at once. It fails in order.

At 9:14 on a Tuesday, the lights go out. Not all of them. The kitchen first. Then the fridge stops humming, and the quiet is the first thing you notice. You check your phone. Six missed messages. You look outside. The whole street is dark.

---

This is not your fuse box. Every window across the road is empty too, so the problem is somewhere else, and nobody is coming to fix it tonight. That is the only useful thing you will learn in the first ten minutes: this is big.

---

Here is the first clock, and it is already running. Your fridge holds cold for about four hours if you keep the door shut. Everyone opens the door. To look. To think. Every time you open it, you spend the cold. A full freezer is different. Forty eight hours, as long as you leave it alone.

---

The tap still runs. Notice that. Water pressure lives in towers and pumps, and it does not end when the lights do. But it ends, usually within a day, sometimes within hours. So this is your window. Fill everything now. Bottles, jugs, the bathtub, the pots. One gallon per person, per day, is the number that decides how the rest of the week goes.

---

Your phone still works. That is temporary too. Towers run on batteries and generators, and they have hours, not days. Text instead of calling. Text gets through when the network is too busy for voices. Send one message: where you are, that you are safe. Then stop. Stop scrolling. Save the battery.

---

The roads are not closed. They are worse. Traffic lights are dead, so every junction becomes a four way argument. And everyone who drives out to look is one more car between you and the way home. So do not go and look. Stay put.

---

Card readers need power. Cash does not. The machines in the wall are dead, but a note in your pocket still buys things. This is the last hour it will be easy, so if you keep cash in the house, keep it now.

---

Then it gets dark, and the real danger arrives and it is not what anyone expects. It is a candle next to a curtain, with no water pressure to put it out. It is a grill brought inside for warmth. It is a generator in the garage, with the door open, thinking that is enough. Carbon monoxide is the reason the first night kills. Outdoors. Twenty feet from every door and window. Always.

---

The house does not go cold all at once either. Brick, glass and furniture hold heat for hours, which is why night one is uncomfortable and not yet dangerous. Pick one small room. Close its door. Stay in it.

---

In the morning, the fridge has lost. Four hours have passed. It is not cold any more. Do not taste it to check. Smell is not a test. Anything that was cold and is now warm goes out. The freezer is still holding. Leave it closed.

---

Nothing dramatic happens on the first night. That is exactly what makes it dangerous. The grid does not fail all at once. It fails in order. Learn the order, and the first night costs you nothing but a dark evening. Night two is the one that gets people. See you on night two.
