# Verified source list — "Why Did Humans Start Cooking?"

All numbers used in the narration resolve to one of these. Full list goes
in the YouTube description (youtube.txt). Verified 2026-10-27.

1. Koebnick C, Strassner C, Hoffmann I, Leitzmann C. "Consequences of a
   long-term raw food diet on body weight and menstruation: results of a
   questionnaire survey." *Annals of Nutrition and Metabolism* 43(2):69–79
   (1999). 216 men + 297 women; mean loss 9.9 kg (men) / 12 kg (women);
   25.0% of women underweight; ~30% of women under 45 partial or complete
   amenorrhoea. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10436305/

2. Aiello LC, Wheeler P. "The Expensive-Tissue Hypothesis: The Brain and
   the Digestive System in Human and Primate Evolution." *Current
   Anthropology* 36(2):199–221 (1995). Gut/brain metabolic trade-off.

3. Wrangham R. *Catching Fire: How Cooking Made Us Human.* Basic Books
   (2009). Cooking hypothesis; cooking placed near the appearance of
   *Homo erectus* ~1.8 Ma; cooking is universal and unique to humans.
   https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2009/06/invention-of-cooking-drove-evolution-of-the-human-species-new-book-argues/

4. Carmody RN, Wrangham RW. "The energetic significance of cooking."
   *Journal of Human Evolution* 57(4):379–391 (2009). Median increase in
   net energy gained from cooked starch ≈30.2%; starch gelatinisation,
   protein denaturation, pathogen kill.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19732938/
   PDF: https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstreams/7312037c-8699-6bd4-e053-0100007df3b/download

5. Carmody RN, Weintraub GS, Wrangham RW. "Energetic consequences of
   thermal and nonthermal food processing." *PNAS* 108(48) (2011). Mice
   fed cooked meat maintained body mass on less food; mice on raw diets
   lost weight. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1112128108

6. Fonseca-Azevedo K, Herculano-Houzel S. "Metabolic constraint imposes
   tradeoff between body size and number of brain neurons in human
   evolution." *PNAS* 109(45):18571–18576 (2012). On a raw diet, Homo
   species would need >9 h/day of feeding to afford their neuron counts.
   https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1206390109

7. Organ C, Nunn CL, Machanda Z, Wrangham RW. "Phylogenetic rate shifts
   in feeding time during the evolution of Homo." *PNAS* 108(35):14555–14559
   (2011). Humans spend 4.7% of daily activity feeding vs 48% predicted
   by phylogeny and body mass. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1107806108

8. Zink KD, Lieberman DE. "Impact of meat and Lower Palaeolithic food
   processing techniques on chewing in humans." *Nature* 531:500–503
   (2016). Slicing meat and pounding tubers: ~17% fewer chews, ~26% less
   masticatory force, ~2.5 million fewer chews per year.
   https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16990

9. Roebroeks W, Villa P. "On the earliest evidence for habitual use of
   fire in Europe." *PNAS* 108(13):5209–5214 (2011). Argues habitual fire
   in Europe appears only ~400,000 years ago.
   https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1018116108

10. Berna F, Goldberg P, Kolska Horwitz L, Brink J, Holt S, Bamford M,
    Chazan M. "Microstratigraphic evidence of in situ fire in the
    Acheulean strata of Wonderwerk Cave, Northern Cape province, South
    Africa." *PNAS* 109(20):E1215–E1220 (2012). Wood ash and burned bone
    ~30 m inside the cave, heated to ~500 °C, layer ~1.0 Ma.
    https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109

11. Alperson-Afil N, Goren-Inbar N (and the GBY spatial analyses).
    Gesher Benot Ya'aqov, Israel, ~790,000 years old: clusters of burned
    flint interpreted as repeated hearths.
    https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/692721

12. Marin-Monfort MD, Shaw CL, Natalio F, Grossman L, Andrews P, Campos J,
    García-Morato S, Pereira JM, Pons A, Chazan M, Kolska Horwitz L,
    Fernández-Jalvo Y. "New evidence for Early Pleistocene use of fire at
    Wonderwerk Cave (South Africa)." *PLOS One* 21(6):e0347480 (2026).
    Burned micromammal bones in Stratum 11, 1.07–1.79 Ma; fire repeatedly
    brought deep into the cave; does not demonstrate routine cooking.
    https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480

13. Wiessner PW. "Embers of society: Firelight talk among the Ju/'hoansi
    Bushmen." *PNAS* 111(39):14027–14035 (2014). 174 conversations; day:
    31% economic, 34% complaint/criticism, 6% stories; night: 81% stories,
    4% economic, 7% complaint/criticism.
    https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1404212111
